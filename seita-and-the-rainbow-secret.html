🫖 ✨ Chapter One

Seita, Who Notices Everything

Seita lived in a teacup on the castle windowsill, and had, for as long as anyone could remember, which in fairyland was a surprisingly long time for a creature so small. He was a prawn, pink and dramatic, prone to sudden flips and theatrical sighs, and he noticed absolutely everything that happened within sight of his window, which was most things, since Fairy kept the castle's best view for him on purpose.

Fairy and Princess Lauren visited him most mornings, hand in hand down the hall and out onto the sill. "Morning, Seita," Fairy would say, and Seita would answer with a flip, sometimes proud, sometimes dramatic, always exactly the flip the morning called for.

But lately, Seita had been carrying something heavier than usual, tucked somewhere behind all those flips. He had known a secret for a very long time now, Euna's secret, the real reason behind her magic, and it had never once bothered him to keep it. Secrets kept out of love were easy to carry, he always said, whenever anyone asked. Lately, though, it had started to feel less easy, because Princess Lauren kept wondering out loud about Euna, and every single time she did, Seita had to very carefully not answer.

🌈 Chapter Two

The Woods Gone a Little Dim

One afternoon, walking hand in hand along the path that bordered the Rainbow Woods, Fairy stopped, and looked for a long moment, and did not say anything right away, which was unusual enough that Princess Lauren noticed immediately.

"What is it?" she asked.

"The colors," Fairy said slowly. "They're a little dimmer than they used to be. Just a little. You would have to know them well to notice at all." He did know them well, though, every shade of every color in that wood, and he was certain.

🌈 What Fairy Noticed

Every color in the Rainbow Woods, just slightly dimmer than it should be. Nobody else had caught it yet.

Princess Lauren looked harder, and once Fairy had pointed it out, she thought she could see it too, the reds and blues and golds all sitting just slightly further back from themselves than they should, like a song sung a half note under where it belonged. "Can we fix it?" she asked.

"I don't know yet," Fairy admitted. "But I intend to find out." He squeezed her hand, and they kept walking, a little more thoughtfully than before.

Chapter Three

A Question Seita Almost Answered

That evening, Princess Lauren sat on the windowsill beside Seita's teacup, swinging her feet, working up to a question she had asked a hundred different ways over the years without ever quite getting an answer.

"Seita," she said. "What is Euna's magic, really? You know. I know you know."

Seita went very still, which for Seita was its own kind of answer. His antennae drooped, just slightly, the way they did when something mattered more than he wanted it to show. For one long moment it looked very much like he was finally going to tell her.

Then he closed his mouth again, and did a small, apologetic flip instead.

Princess Lauren's face fell, just a little, before she could stop it. Fairy, sitting beside her, took her hand. "He's not being unkind," Fairy said gently. "A secret kept with love is a different thing than a secret kept to hurt somebody. Seita's kind. He's just also loyal, and sometimes those two things ask the same thing of a person at the very same time."

Princess Lauren thought about that, and decided, slowly, that she could wait a little longer, even though waiting was not her favorite thing in the world. Seita, from his teacup, looked more grateful than she had ever seen him look, over something so small as simply being allowed to keep a promise.

🌙 Chapter Four

What DanDan Said in the Dark

That night, long after Princess Lauren had gone to sleep, Seita heard a small voice in the dark beside his teacup, exactly where no one was standing.

"The Woods are dimming," DanDan said. "I have noticed it too. Have you?"

"I notice everything," Seita said. "It is sort of my whole job."

DanDan was quiet for a moment, turning something over, the way he did when a gem or a thought needed careful handling. "I think it might be time," he said finally. "Not because the secret was ever wrong to keep. Because the Woods need it now, and Euna would want to help, if she knew how much."

"She already knows," came a third voice, soft and unhurried, from the saucer beside Seita's cup. Euna had been there the whole time, tucked in her shell, listening the way she listened to everything. "I have known for a while. I was only waiting to see who would ask first."

Seita looked at her, and then at the space where DanDan's voice had come from, and felt the heaviness he had been carrying for so long shift, just slightly, toward something lighter. "Fairy and Lauren should see it properly," he said. "Not just hear about it. See it."

Euna agreed that sounded exactly right, and the three of them, two visible and one not, began making a plan for the very next clear night.

🌙 ⭐ Chapter Five

The Trail at Night

The next clear night, Seita rode out to the edge of the Rainbow Woods in his teacup, carried carefully by Fairy, with Princess Lauren walking alongside, hand in Fairy's the whole way, wide awake well past her bedtime for an occasion Seita had promised was worth it.

They settled at the tree line just as the last of the light gave out, and waited, and watched.

Euna came slowly, the way Euna did everything, making her way along the path at the very edge of the Woods, tucked in her shell, in no hurry at all. And as she moved, impossibly slow and impossibly certain, her trail began to glimmer behind her, soft at first, then brighter, then blooming up into a long, low ribbon of color that spilled across the grass and up into the leaves, red and gold and green and blue and violet, all at once, all exactly where they needed to be.

🐌 Euna's Secret, At Last

Wherever she goes slowly, and out of love, her trail turns to light. That is where the Rainbow Woods gets its color, every single time, and it always has.

Princess Lauren watched, Fairy's hand tight in hers, until the whole stretch of woods in front of them was glowing warm and full and exactly as bright as it should always have been. "That's the secret," she whispered, like saying it any louder might scare it off. "That's really it."

"That's really it," Fairy agreed, just as quietly. Neither of them let go of the other's hand the entire time, not even once.

Chapter Six

Why Seita Never Told

Afterward, walking back to the castle with the Woods glowing bright behind them, Princess Lauren finally asked the question underneath all her other questions. "Why did it have to be a secret at all? It is the nicest thing I have ever seen."

Seita considered this from his teacup, swaying gently with Fairy's steps. "Because Euna never wanted to be watched doing it," he said. "Some kindness only works if nobody is keeping score, or waiting around to say thank you. The moment it becomes a performance, it stops being quite so true."

Princess Lauren thought that was one of the wisest things she had ever heard, and told him so, which made Seita do the proudest flip of his entire life, teacup and all.

"You kept it a very long time," Fairy said to him, warmly. "That could not have been easy."

"It wasn't," Seita admitted. "But it was worth it. And it is nice, now, not to have to carry it alone anymore."

✦ ✦ ✦

And so, from that night on, the secret was simply a story the way everything else in fairyland eventually became a story, known and loved and told again whenever the Woods needed remembering. Princess Lauren held Fairy's hand the whole way home, same as always, and Seita rode along in his teacup behind them, glowing with the particular satisfaction of a secret well kept and finally, gently, let go.
