A New Chapter of Fairyland
Chapter One

The Snail Who Keeps a Secret

Down by the teacup window, where Seita had lived for as long as anyone could remember, there lived a snail named Euna. She was small and slow and perfectly content to stay that way, and most of the time you would only ever see the curve of her shell, tucked in tight, waiting patiently for whatever came next.

Everyone in fairyland agreed that Euna was magical. That much was obvious, the way it is obvious that a star is bright even before you know exactly what it is made of. But how her magic worked, and what it actually did, was a secret that only two creatures in all of fairyland were allowed to know. Seita, because he was her oldest friend and had never once told a secret in his life, prawn or otherwise. And DanDan, because he was invisible already, and Euna said an invisible friend was the safest place in the world to keep a secret.

Fairy and Princess Lauren stopped by the windowsill most mornings, hand in hand, just to say good morning to Seita and to the small shy shell beside him. Princess Lauren had asked, more than once, what exactly Euna's magic was. Fairy always gave the same answer. "Not ours to know," he'd say, giving her hand a squeeze. "But you can keep asking nicely. Maybe someday." Princess Lauren always did, and Euna always just tucked in a little tighter, pleased to be asked so kindly and in no hurry to answer.

🐌 What Fairyland Knows About Euna

She is magical. That part everyone agrees on. The rest, only Seita and DanDan know, and neither one is telling.

Chapter Two

An Ordinary Afternoon at the Park

On an ordinary afternoon, the kind fairyland had plenty of, Fairy took Princess Lauren and LittleLove to the little park at the edge of the meadow, the one with the crooked swing and the pond just beyond it. Seita came too, riding along in his teacup the way he preferred to travel, and Euna rode beside him on a small white saucer, tucked into her shell and perfectly happy to be carried.

Fairy set the teacup and the saucer down on the flat stone by the swings, side by side, exactly the way Seita liked it. "You've got the best seats in the house," Fairy told them both, and Seita did a small proud flip that sloshed a little tea over the rim, which nobody minded in the slightest.

Princess Lauren settled into the grass beside the stone, still holding Fairy's hand, and looked out at the whole bright afternoon spread in front of them, the swings and the pond and the gum tree beyond. "This is my favorite kind of day," she said. Fairy sat down next to her, hand still in hers. "Mine too," he said. "Ordinary ones are the best kind, if you ask me."

Chapter Three

The Kookaburra Who Laughs at Everything

Far off at the edge of the park, in the branches of a gum tree that leaned out over everything like it was keeping watch, sat an old wise kookaburra. He had been laughing, on and off, for as long as anyone could remember, and nobody had ever quite figured out what was so funny.

He laughed when Princess Lauren tripped over nothing at all and laughed harder when she laughed right back at herself, still holding tight to Fairy's hand so she didn't quite fall. He laughed when the wind took LittleLove's hat clean off his head. He laughed, mostly, because laughing was simply what old wise kookaburras did with an afternoon this good, and fairyland had long since decided his laugh was one of the nicest sounds in the whole park, better than any song, better even than DanDan's gems clicking together.

Fairy tipped his head back and laughed right along with the kookaburra, and Princess Lauren laughed because Fairy was laughing, and the two of them stood there a while just laughing at nothing in particular, which is one of the very best things two people who love each other can do on a sunny afternoon.

Chapter Four

Seita's Dance

Somewhere around the middle of the afternoon, with the kookaburra laughing steady in the background, Seita decided the teacup needed dancing in, and so he danced in it. It was not a graceful dance, exactly, all quick little turns and one dramatic flip that splashed tea onto the stone, but it was enthusiastic, and Euna, tucked in her shell on the saucer beside him, gave the smallest shiver of a laugh that only Seita ever noticed.

"You're going to fall out of that cup one day," Euna said, in the quiet way she said most things.

"Probably," Seita agreed, and kept dancing anyway.

Fairy and Princess Lauren clapped along from the grass, keeping time badly and not caring in the slightest. "Encore!" Princess Lauren called out, and Seita, delighted to have an audience that appreciated him properly, did the whole dance again, tea and all, while Fairy squeezed Princess Lauren's hand in time with the splashing.

🎶 The Sound of the Afternoon

A kookaburra laughing, a prawn dancing in a teacup, and somewhere close by, a snail almost laughing too.

Chapter Five

Madge, Floating

Just beyond the swings, in the little pond that caught all the afternoon light, Madge floated on her back, ancient and unhurried, eyes half closed, in no rush to be anywhere else at all. She had come to watch the children play, the way she always did, and to be near enough that if anyone needed her slow, sure kind of wisdom, she would be right there.

Fairy and Princess Lauren wandered over to the pond's edge to say hello, her hand still in his. "Is it a good day, Madge?" Princess Lauren asked, crouching down at the water. Madge opened one ancient eye, just barely. "It is when the two of you are holding hands," she said, and closed it again, perfectly content.

All was well. That was the thing about Madge floating in the pond on a good afternoon. Fairyland always seemed to settle just a little, right down into itself, whenever Madge decided it was a floating kind of day, and Princess Lauren decided Madge might be the wisest creature in all of fairyland for noticing exactly the right thing.

Chapter Six

Playing with Her New Best Friend

Princess Lauren had never quite gotten the hang of swinging on her own, always needing somebody behind her to push, and it was LittleLove, of all people, who finally offered to teach her properly. He climbed onto the swing beside hers to show her exactly how it worked, legs pumping forward, then tucking back, timing the lean just right. "I've been practicing," he admitted, a little shy about it even now. "For two months. Just so I'd be good enough to teach you." Princess Lauren decided that might be the sweetest thing anyone had ever practiced for, and told him so, which made him glow so bright he nearly forgot to finish the demonstration.

By the middle of the afternoon she had it, wobbly but real, pumping her own legs and leaning back at exactly the moment LittleLove called out to her. Then the two of them took over the swings for the rest of the day, pushing each other higher and higher and arguing happily about whose turn it was next. LittleLove had come out from behind his tree for this, all the way out, and did not once think about going back to it while there was still swinging to be done. Fairy stood nearby for a while too, pushing her the old way whenever her arms got tired, though she asked for it a good deal less than she used to.

Halfway through the afternoon, a small determined shape came toddling across the grass toward them. Liam had gotten loose from Fairy's other hand for exactly as long as it took him to spot his big sister on the swings, and he was not about to be left out of it. Princess Lauren scooped him up without even slowing down and settled him right on her lap, and LittleLove came around behind them both to push, gentle and careful, the way you push a swing when there are two people on it and one of them is very small. "Lala," Liam said, patting her cheek with one sticky hand, which was the only word he needed for her, and the only one that mattered. LittleLove pushed a little higher for that, just because it seemed like the moment called for it.

Somewhere in the middle of it all, Princess Lauren thought, just briefly, about a boat that might have sailed away once, off toward some far and different land. She was not entirely sure it had. She could not quite remember, and for a moment that not knowing sat strangely in her chest. She reached one hand back off the swing, and Fairy caught it without her even having to ask, holding it steady until the feeling passed.

Then LittleLove called her name from the top of the swing, laughing, and the kookaburra laughed along with him from his branch, and Seita did another dramatic flip in his teacup, and the not knowing did not seem to matter so very much after all. Whatever had or had not sailed away, she had Fairy's hand in hers, her baby brother giggling on her lap, and a new best friend right here, right now, pushing them both higher on the swing, and that was a very good thing to have, maybe the best thing.

Chapter Seven

Gladys, Who Makes Dreams Come True

Deeper in the Rainbow Woods, past the swings and the pond and the gum tree where the kookaburra liked to laugh, there lived a unicorn named Gladys, and everyone in fairyland agreed she was the most mystical thing in the whole wood.

Her horn was five and a half inches long exactly, LittleLove had measured it once, very carefully, and it sparkled and glittered with every step she took, throwing small rainbows across the grass whether she meant to or not. On her rump, just above her tail, she carried a heart shaped rainbow, and her mane and tail were the exact soft pink and blue of spun candy floss at a fair, the kind that comes apart into little clouds when you pull it.

🦄 Wishes and Dreams

Esma grants wishes, the kind you ask for out loud. Gladys tends dreams, the kind that are already yours, and just helps them find their way to you a little sooner.

Gladys was quite firm on that difference, actually. "A wish is something you ask for," she liked to explain, in her low velvet voice. "A dream is something you already have. I don't grant those. I only help them along." Nobody in fairyland argued with her about it twice.

Gladys let three riders come find her, when the afternoon light through the leaves went soft and gold. Princess Lauren went first, because princesses generally do, and held tight to the pink and blue mane while Gladys walked slow, careful circles around the clearing, her horn throwing small rainbows over the grass with every step.

LittleLove went next, delighted and glowing brighter than he had all day, and Gladys walked even slower for him, the way she seemed to know exactly how much bravery each rider had brought along that afternoon.

Liam went last, small and thrilled, both fists knotted happily in the candy floss mane, and Gladys carried him at a gentle, rocking walk while Princess Lauren jogged alongside just in case, though Gladys never once actually needed the help.

But for all her sparkle, Gladys never once stepped past the edge of the Rainbow Woods, not for anyone, not even for the littlest rider. "My magic only works in here," she told them, when Princess Lauren finally asked why. "Out there, I'm only ever a very pretty horse." Nobody believed that for a second, but Gladys never seemed to mind being teased about it, and stayed exactly where she was, glittering, patient, and entirely herself.

Chapter Eight

The Rainbow Woods Playground

And that was the secret about this particular park, the one with the crooked swing and the pond and the gum tree with the laughing kookaburra in it. It sat right at the edge of the Rainbow Woods, and the Rainbow Woods, everyone in fairyland knew, was safe. Truly safe, the kind of safe you did not have to think twice about.

So Fairy let Princess Lauren and LittleLove swing as high as they liked. Seita danced in his teacup until the tea was mostly gone. Euna stayed curled on her saucer, magical and quiet and not telling how. Madge floated. The kookaburra laughed. Gladys grazed nearby, glittering, close enough to be found if anyone needed a slow lap around the clearing. And the whole afternoon went by exactly the easy, unhurried way a good afternoon in the Rainbow Woods is supposed to go, with nothing to worry about at all, and a new best friend to swing beside until the sun started sinking low.

When it was finally time to go, Fairy took Princess Lauren's hand for the walk home, the teacup and the saucer riding along behind them, and she talked the whole way about the kookaburra and the swings and Seita's encore, until the castle came into view and the day settled quietly into evening, her hand still in his the entire way.
