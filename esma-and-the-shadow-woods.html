A New Chapter of Fairyland
🌙 ⭐ Chapter One

The Third Wood

Every fairyland has its rules, and by now everyone knew the two woods well enough. The Yellow Woods, where you never went, not once, not ever. The Green Woods, where you listened close and stayed careful and were looked after in return.

But there was a third wood, and it did not appear on any map, because it did not stay in one place long enough to be mapped.

Princess Lauren learned about it the way she learned most things in fairyland, by walking hand in hand with Fairy until a question found her all on its own. "Where does that path go?" she asked one evening, pointing to a place where the light seemed to bend a little differently than everywhere else. Fairy stopped walking and held her hand a little tighter. "That is the third wood," Fairy said. "Come sit with me, and I will tell you about it."

🌫 The Shadow Woods... The In Between

It was not dangerous the way the Yellow Woods was dangerous, and it was not simply wise the way the Green Woods was wise. It was something else entirely. The Shadow Woods was where people and animals went when they were finished with one home and not yet arrived at the next one. Some were on their way up to heaven, walking slow and unafraid, taking their time to look back at everything they loved. Others were only passing through, on their way back to the ones who were waiting for them, worried, with the porch light on.

Nobody in fairyland was frightened of the Shadow Woods, not really. They spoke of it softly, the way you speak of something important. Fairy said the trees there had no shadows of their own, being made of shadow already, quiet and silver and gentle as dusk. And if you stood very still at the edge of it, you could sometimes hear something like a door opening, far off, and then very softly, closing again behind whoever had just gone through.

Princess Lauren listened to every word with her head resting against Fairy's arm, and when Fairy finished, she thought about it for a long moment before she spoke. "I'm not scared," she said, "if you're not." Fairy smiled the particular smile a queen saves for a princess who has just said exactly the right thing, and held her hand the whole way back to the castle, past the pond, past the garden, all the way to the door.

Chapter Two

Esma, Who Grants Wishes

Not far from the edge of the Shadow Woods lived a rabbit named Esma. She had soft grey fur and two long ears that swiveled toward anything interesting, and eyes the color of new spring leaves, a bright and certain emerald green that seemed to hold a small light of their own.

Esma could grant wishes. Not big loud wishes with fireworks and trumpets, but small true ones, the kind that matter most. And the way you knew a wish was coming true was this: her little pink nose would crinkle, just once, quick as a blink, and whatever had been wished for, quietly and honestly, from the very middle of somebody's heart, would begin, somehow, to come true.

"You'll like her," Fairy told Princess Lauren, leading her down the path by the hand. "She doesn't grant every wish, mind you. Only the ones that mean something." Princess Lauren thought about that the whole way there, trying to decide which of her wishes meant something and which ones were only ordinary wanting.

🐇 How to Tell a Wish Is Coming True

Esma's nose crinkles once. Quick, small, and certain. Blink and you might miss it, but the wish never does.

She did not grant every wish. Wishing does not work that way, and Esma was far too wise a rabbit to pretend otherwise. But if a wish was kind, and true, and needed, her nose would crinkle without her even deciding to let it, and fairyland would shift, just slightly, to make room for it.

Esma lived close to the Shadow Woods on purpose. Someone gentle needed to be nearby, she said, for anyone passing through who might feel a little lost, or a little scared, or might simply want one small good thing to wish for on their way.

When Fairy and Princess Lauren finally reached her, Esma looked up at the two of them standing together, hands linked, and something about the sight of them made her nose crinkle before either one had wished for a single thing. "That one wasn't for you," Esma said, when Princess Lauren asked. "That one was just because I like seeing the two of you together. Some wishes grant themselves."

Chapter Three

Sometimes Andy Bean, Always a Queen

Now, there is something about Fairy that must be explained properly, because it confuses newcomers to fairyland every single time.

Sometimes Fairy went by Fairy, wings out, wand ready, exactly as everyone in the old stories remembered. But sometimes, especially on quiet mornings or when only the closest friends were around, Fairy went by a different name entirely. Andy Bean.

Nobody could tell you exactly why, and Fairy never seemed to think it needed explaining. Andy Bean was every bit as brave as Fairy, every bit as kind, and every bit as fond of ice cream and terrible jokes and holding Princess Lauren's hand on the path home. The wings came and went, and the name came and went with them.

Princess Lauren asked about it once, walking beside Fairy near the edge of the Shadow Woods, where the light always seemed to make her curious about everything. "Which one are you today?" she asked. Fairy thought about it for a moment, turning her hand over gently in his own. "Whichever one you need," he said. "Today I think I'm just yours. Call me whichever name feels right." Princess Lauren decided that was a very good answer, and did not ask again for a long while.

Names got her thinking, though, the way one big question always led to a handful of smaller ones. "Why is Boat Momma a boat and a donut," she asked, a few minutes later, entirely out of nowhere, "and not a ladybug, when she's a lady, and a bug too, some mornings, before coffee?" Fairy considered this with all the seriousness it deserved. "I genuinely don't have an answer for that," he admitted. "You'll have to write to her about it." Princess Lauren said she absolutely would, and, being five, possibly never did.

✦ ✦ ✦

But one thing never changed, no matter which name was being used that day. Fairy was a queen. Always. Whether flying as Fairy in a crown of gold, or walking as Andy Bean with hands in pockets and no crown in sight at all, the queen underneath was the very same one, steady and certain, the way the moon is still the moon whether you can see it that night or not.

"You're my favorite, either way," Princess Lauren told him, and Fairy laughed, the warm easy laugh that meant he believed her completely, and squeezed her hand once, twice, the way he always did when a thing was too true to need saying twice out loud.

Chapter Four

The Meeting at Madge's Pond

Esma first came to the castle garden on an ordinary afternoon, hopping along the path with her ears low and thoughtful. Madge was there first, as Madge always was, sitting beside her clear little pond with her ancient starry eyes half closed.

"You are the wishing rabbit," Madge said, not a question, the way turtles who have lived a very long time tend to say things.

"I am," said Esma, and her nose crinkled just slightly, out of habit, though she had not wished for anything at all.

One by one the others gathered, the way they always did in fairyland when something new and good had arrived. DanDan announced himself with a small shower of light where no one was standing, and a tiny rose colored gem appeared in the grass at Esma's feet as a kind of hello. Princess Lauren came running down from the meadow with her crown slightly crooked, delighted, already asking a hundred questions at once. Locks arrived more slowly, quietly beautiful as always, and sat close enough to the castle wall that LittleLove could peek out from behind it without having to come too far. And Seita, who had been napping in his teacup by the window, climbed up onto the sill just to see what the fuss was about, and did one small dramatic flip of approval when he spotted the rabbit with the emerald eyes.

Fairy caught up to Princess Lauren just as she reached the pond, out of breath and still talking, and took her hand to slow her down. "One question at a time," Fairy said, laughing. "Esma isn't going anywhere." He knelt beside her at the water's edge and made the introduction properly, the way he always did, with Princess Lauren's hand still in his the whole time. "Esma, this is Princess Lauren, who asks the best questions in all of fairyland. Lauren, this is Esma, who answers the ones that matter."

Chapter Five

DanDan's Gems Light the Path

It did not take long for the friends to learn what Esma already knew, that living so close to the Shadow Woods meant something interesting happened almost every week.

One evening, Fairy and Princess Lauren were walking near the edge of the woods, her hand tucked into his as always, when DanDan came back without his favorite gem, a deep blue sapphire he had carried since before anyone could remember. He had dropped it while helping someone find their way through, he explained, someone small and frightened who had wandered in without meaning to and could not remember which direction was home.

"I gave them the sapphire to hold," DanDan said, and though nobody could see his face, everyone could hear that he was smiling. "Light helps. Even light you cannot keep."

Princess Lauren squeezed Fairy's hand at that, and looked up at him. "That's the kindest thing I've ever heard," she said. Fairy nodded, and did not let go of her hand for the rest of the walk. "That's DanDan for you," he said. "Give him a gem and he'll give it away twice before breakfast."

Esma's nose crinkled at that, quick and certain, and by morning a new gem had appeared at the edge of the Shadow Woods, waiting for DanDan exactly where the old one had been lost. Fairy, who was going by Andy Bean that particular morning, said that was simply how the Shadow Woods worked. What you gave away kindly there had a way of finding its way back to you, just a little different, and just as bright. Princess Lauren decided she liked that rule best of all, and said so, out loud, holding Fairy's hand the whole way home.

Chapter Six

Princess Lauren, Locks, and LittleLove

Princess Lauren was not afraid of the Shadow Woods either, though she admitted, quietly, holding Fairy's hand, that it made her feel something bigger than fear. Something more like missing someone before you have even said goodbye to them.

"Boat Momma sailed a long way once," she said one evening, watching the silver trees at the edge of the dark. "Not through there. But it made me think of it. Going somewhere far, and trusting it's the right somewhere."

Fairy squeezed her hand and said nothing, because some things do not need an answer, only a hand to hold while you think them. After a while he spoke anyway, quietly. "You don't have to figure it all out tonight," he said. "You just have to know that wherever you're going, and wherever I'm going, we're holding hands the whole way there." Princess Lauren leaned her head against his arm and decided that was enough of an answer after all.

Locks, who loved the castle completely, took to leaving a lantern lit in the highest window every night, just in case anyone passing through the Shadow Woods needed one small warm light to walk toward. And LittleLove, shy as ever, found the courage to glow a little brighter near the woods' edge than anywhere else in fairyland, without anyone even having to ask. Some fears get smaller, Locks said, when you turn them into something useful instead.

Chapter Seven

Seita's Wish

Even Seita, who mostly kept his opinions to himself, had something to say about the Shadow Woods, once Esma finally understood him. Fairy and Princess Lauren happened to be visiting that afternoon, sitting together on the windowsill beside his teacup, and so they were the ones who heard it first.

It turned out Seita had a wish of his own, small and specific the way a prawn's wishes tend to be. He wished, more than anything, that whoever passed through the Shadow Woods might have something warm to look forward to on the other side, whichever side that turned out to be.

Princess Lauren, still holding Fairy's hand, leaned in close to the teacup to listen better. "That might be the best wish I've ever heard," she whispered to Fairy. Fairy agreed, and told Esma so directly, which made Esma's ears go a little pink with the compliment of it.

🦐 Seita's Wish

That whoever walks through finds something warm waiting for them. On any side.

Esma's nose crinkled at that too, longer this time, the way it did for wishes made on behalf of somebody else instead of yourself, which Esma always said were the strongest kind. From that day on, warmth seemed to follow the path through the Shadow Woods just a little more reliably than it had before, though nobody could ever quite explain why, except that a prawn in a teacup had wished for it once, sincerely, and Esma had been listening.

Fairy carried Princess Lauren home on his shoulders that evening, both of them talking about Seita's wish the whole way, and Princess Lauren decided, out loud and with great certainty, that she wanted to make a wish like that too, someday. "One that isn't for me," she said. Fairy told her she already had, more than once, and just hadn't noticed.

Chapter Eight

The Lost Little One Finds Home

One night, a very small sound came from the edge of the Shadow Woods, a sound like someone trying very hard not to cry and not quite managing it.

It was a little firefly, barely lit at all, who had wandered in from somewhere far off and could not remember which direction led home and which direction led onward. Fairy, as Andy Bean that night, went in first, unafraid, because a queen does not let a small frightened light spend one more minute alone in the dark. Madge came next, slow and certain, because the very old always know the way better than they let on. DanDan's gems lit the path in soft colors, and Locks's lantern glowed steady in the highest window, and LittleLove's glow reached further into the dark than it ever had before. Princess Lauren stood at the very edge, holding both hands out, ready. And Seita, from his teacup, did his small dramatic flip, just to let the little firefly know that somebody, somewhere, was cheering for it.

Esma walked all the way in, right up beside the firefly, and asked it very gently what it wished for.

"Home," the firefly said. Just that. "I wish I could find my way home."

🐇 The Crinkle

Esma's nose crinkled once, quick and sure and full of certainty. And somewhere close by, a small warm light turned on, in a window, in a place that had been waiting.

The firefly felt it before it saw it, a pull like a hand reaching back for its hand, and it followed that pull out of the Shadow Woods, past Princess Lauren's open hands, past Locks's lantern, out into the ordinary night, home.

Princess Lauren watched it go with both hands still cupped where it had been, and then reached one of them out for Fairy's instead. He took it without a word, and the two of them stood at the edge of the Shadow Woods a while longer, just to be sure, before walking back to the castle together, the same way they always walked everywhere.

Chapter Eight and a Half

Franklin, and the Tree Whose Leaves Had Fallen

At the very edge of the Shadow Woods stood a tree unlike any other in fairyland. All summer long its branches had been full and green, the softest place in the whole garden to nap in the shade. Then one quiet morning, without any wind at all, every single leaf let go at once, and drifted down slow and gentle, and settled in a warm gold circle all around its roots.

It was there, curled up in that very circle, that fairyland found Franklin.

Franklin was the oldest, gentlest dog anyone in fairyland had ever known, so old that counting the years the ordinary way did not seem fair to how much he had loved in that time. He had gray around his nose, and he slept most afternoons in warm patches of sun, and one morning he simply did not wake the way he always had. He had only walked, quietly and without any fuss at all, into that circle of fallen leaves, and from there, the same gentle way the little firefly once had, into the Shadow Woods.

🍂 What the Tree Knows

Leaves falling does not mean a tree is gone. It only means something in it has finished, and something new is already getting ready to grow. Come spring, this same tree stands full and green again, same as it always has.

Princess Lauren found out at bedtime, and everything in her went very still, and then all at once, very loud. This was how it always went with her, quiet first, and then the words coming fast, one right on top of the other, question tumbling into question before the last one had even landed. Fairy had learned a long time ago not to try to slow her down when she got like that, and not to interrupt either. You let her get all the way through it. That was simply how she worked a big feeling loose.

"Where did he go?" she asked Fairy, though it was not really a question. It was more like she was checking that the ground was still there.

"Into the Shadow Woods," Fairy said, sitting down beside her so they were the very same height. "The in between we've talked about. He wasn't scared, Lauren. He lived a long, good life, full of naps and walks and being loved completely, and when it was time, he simply walked to wherever he needed to go next."

Princess Lauren thought about that for a long moment, turning it over the way she turned over every big thing before she let it settle. "Is that why it's called the in between?" she asked. "Because you get to choose? Stay, or go?"

Fairy nodded slowly. "Something like that. Nobody decides for you. When it's really and truly time, you get to choose your own way through."

"Franklin chose to go," she said.

"He did," Fairy said. "Gently, and on his own terms, surrounded by everyone who loved him."

🐇 Esma's Crinkle, One More Time

Not far off, Esma's nose crinkled, soft and certain, for an old dog who had wished for nothing more than one warm place to land. Seita had already wished for exactly that, once, sincerely. Whichever side Franklin was on now, something warm was waiting.

Then Princess Lauren went quiet again, and reached over, and took Fairy's hand in both of hers, holding on tighter than usual. "Are you okay?" she asked him. Not herself. Him.

Fairy looked down at their hands, a little surprised, and a lot touched. "I'm okay," he said. "I promise. Right here, right now, I'm okay."

"But what if something happens to you," she said, "and I need to hold your hand and you're not there to hold it back?"

Fairy did not rush the answer, because it was too important to rush. "Then you hold onto everyone else who loves you, the same way you're holding onto me right now. And you remember that I am always, always trying to get back to you. That has never once changed, and it never will."

Not far off, curled close to where Franklin used to sleep, another dog was having a hard evening of her own. Pearl kept almost walking over to lie down in his usual spot, and then stopping, and then backing away again, not quite sure what to do with a space that still smelled like him.

Princess Lauren noticed, the way she noticed everything that mattered, and went and sat down right beside Pearl in the grass. "You don't have to be brave about it," she told her, wrapping both arms around Pearl's neck. "I'm not being very brave either." Then, because it seemed like the sort of thing that needed saying out loud, she added, "I'm going to take care of you. Forever. I promise."

Pearl leaned her whole weight into the hug, and that was answer enough.

✦ ✦ ✦

They sat together under the tree with no leaves left, her hand in his, until the sky went the soft purple color that meant it was really time for bed. And right where they sat, if you looked closely enough, one single new bud had already started, small and green and certain, at the very tip of the lowest branch.

"It's already growing back," Princess Lauren whispered.

"It always does," Fairy said, and held her hand the whole way home.

Chapter Nine

The Rules of the Woods, Now Three

And so the rules of fairyland were written again, this time with a third wood added beside the other two. Fairy sat Princess Lauren down on the garden bench to go over them properly, the way he did every so often, her hands in his, so that she would always know exactly where things stood.

⚠ The Yellow Woods... Stay Out

Still avoided, still unexplained, still exactly as unwelcoming as it had always been. Fairy never went there. Not once. Not ever.

🌿 The Green Woods... Tread Carefully

Still wise, still watching out for anyone who paid attention. Always listen in the Green Woods.

🌫 The Shadow Woods... Walk Gently

Not frightening, not final, only in between. Bring a wish if you have one. Bring a friend if you can. Esma is never far from the edge of it.

"And if I ever have to walk through it," Princess Lauren said, "will you come with me?" Fairy did not hesitate. "Every single time," he said. "That one isn't even a rule. That one's just a promise." Princess Lauren decided a promise was better than a rule anyway, and made him say it twice, just to be sure it was real.

Chapter Ten

Every Evening in Fairyland Now

And so, every evening in fairyland now, this is how things are. Fairy, whether flying as Fairy or walking as Andy Bean, comes home the same way, always a queen underneath it all. Madge waits by the pond, older and wiser with every wish granted nearby. DanDan's gems glitter a little brighter near the Shadow Woods than anywhere else, because that is where they matter most.

Princess Lauren's hand finds Fairy's hand on the path home, same as always, and neither one of them ever quite lets go until they reach the door. Some evenings they stop at the edge of the Shadow Woods first, just to look at it together for a moment, not out of fear but out of something closer to respect, the way you look at anything that matters. Locks keeps her lantern lit in the highest window, and LittleLove glows steady at the edge of the dark, and Seita does one small dramatic flip every evening, just because he can.

And Esma, the wishing rabbit with the emerald eyes, sits close to the edge of the Shadow Woods, ears soft and listening, waiting for whoever might need one small true wish on their way through, to heaven, or to home, whichever way they happen to be going.

Her nose crinkles more evenings than not. Fairyland, everyone agrees, is better for it.

The Colors of Loving Fairy

a poem, said all in one breath

Red is for my heart,

it goes thump thump thump.

Orange is for sunset, when Fairy flies home.

Yellow is for the woods you stay out of,

I remembered, see, I did.

Green is for Esma's eyes,

and for the wood that's careful,

and green is for your ice cream too, my favorite kind.

Blue is for your wings,

and blue is for the deep Australian sky.

Purple is for the Shadow Woods,

it isn't scary, Esma said so, and I believe her.

Pink is for Princess Lauren's cheeks,

and gold, gold is for your crown.

And I know you're Fairy,

and I know you're Andy Bean too,

and I know underneath it all you are always my queen.

So that's all the colors,

that's all of them, every one,

and I saved the best word for last because that's how you're supposed to do it.

I love you, Fairy.

🌙 ⭐ ✨ A Poem by Lauren

I'll Always Hold Your Hand

said fast, before I forget the middle part

Hands are for holding,

that's the whole point of hands,

otherwise why would you have five fingers and five more on the other one.

Franklin doesn't have hands,

he has paws,

but Pearl still knows what holding means, so it isn't really about the hands part.

If I count to one hundred and thirty,

that's how old Franklin was, in dog years,

I forget which kind of years, but it's a lot of them.

I'm five.

That's not thirty and it's not one hundred and thirty,

so I have time, but I'm holding your hand anyway, just in case.

If you ever have to go through the Shadow Woods,

I'm not scared of the trees,

I'm scared of my hand being empty, so don't let go first. I'll let go first if I have to, but not you.

Esma says a wish has to be true to count,

so here is a true one, not a made up one.

I wish I get to hold your hand

until I'm as old as Franklin, and then some more after that.

That's it, that's the whole poem,

I said it fast so I wouldn't forget the middle part,

but I didn't forget it, I never forget the important parts.

I'll always hold your hand.

That's not a wish, actually.

That one's a promise,

and I already made you say it twice, so it's real.
